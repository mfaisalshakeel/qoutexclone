- **2026-09-17**: Phase 2 — **Hotkeys**. Configurable shortcuts for higher, lower, amount ±, expiry ±, and next/previous market, with a help overlay on `?`. `web/src/lib/hotkeys.ts` is pure data and pure functions, so the binding shown in the overlay, the one stored in the browser and the one matched against a keystroke are the same thing.

  **The rule that matters most is that a shortcut never fires while someone is typing.** The ticket has a number field, and a platform where typing "25" places two trades is worse than one with no shortcuts at all — so any editable target, anything nested inside one, and `contenteditable` all keep their own keys, and an e2e spec types into the stake field with the step key and asserts nothing moved and no position opened. Defaults avoid keys the browser already owns: WASD for the ticket, brackets for the market list, nothing needing a modifier, no arrows.

  Rebinding captures the next keystroke rather than asking anyone to type a key name, and does it on the capture phase so the key being bound cannot also trigger the shortcut it is replacing. Cmd is read as Ctrl so a Mac binding works; shift is only recorded for non-printable keys, since `shift+/` already produces `?`. Conflicts are detected and named. Preferences live in the trader's browser and survive a reload; one unrecognised entry falls back to that action's default rather than discarding the whole set, and storage being blocked or holding nonsense leaves the defaults intact. `trading.hotkeysEnabled` is the platform switch, and a trader can still turn them off for themselves.

  **A real crash was caught by the linter and then by the suite**: the ticket's imperative handle sat after its early return, so a render where the market had not loaded ran fewer hooks than the next one — "Rendered more hooks than during the previous render", which the Phase 0 error boundary caught and turned into a visible error page rather than a blank app. The handle is now stable and unconditional, forwarding to the actions of whichever render is current.

  19 unit tests for the binding logic, 2 e2e specs. 43 web unit tests now; lint, typecheck, format, build and all 31 e2e specs clean.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 2 — **Ticket UX**. The stake controls are now driven by settings and bounded by the market rather than by hardcoded numbers: presets (`trading.amountPresets`) and the ± step (`trading.amountStep`) come from the registry, and **a preset outside a market's own min/max is not offered for that market** — a trader can never reach a stake that will be refused. Balance-share shortcuts (25/50/75/All) clamp into the same range and disable themselves when the share is below the market's minimum, with the reason on the tooltip. Typing is left alone while it happens and pulled into range on blur, so an intermediate keystroke is never fought, and the range itself is printed under the field. Payout and profit already tracked the stake and the live payout; both are now covered by a spec that checks the profit equals stake × the payout on screen.

  **Repeat and double up** are a server endpoint, `POST /api/trades/:id/repeat`, not a client-side re-submit. The expiry is resolved server-side because by the time anyone clicks, a clock boundary has passed and a duration may no longer be offered: a duration position repeats at the same length if the market still offers it and is refused clearly if not, and a clock position re-buys the soonest boundary still open rather than the one that has gone. It is a new trade, priced and paid at the payout of now, and it goes through every check any other trade does — an integration test confirms a trader who cannot afford the double is refused for insufficient funds. `trading.allowRepeat` turns it off. Both buttons sit on each open position in the terminal.

  7 integration tests (same-trade repeat, the double and its charge, an IDOR check, limits still applying, the operator switch, a withdrawn duration, and the clock re-buy), 2 e2e specs. 204 server + 24 web unit tests, 58 integration tests, lint, typecheck, format and build all clean.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 2 — **Pending trades**. An order that becomes a position when a price level is reached or a time arrives. `engine/orders.ts` is pure and decides the condition; the side of a price level is resolved **when the order is placed**, from the price at that moment, and stored — an order placed above the market fires on the way up, one placed below fires on the way down. Re-deriving the side at execution time would give the wrong answer once the price has crossed and come back, which is exactly the bug that makes this kind of feature untrustworthy. A level sitting exactly on the market is refused as a market order rather than firing on whichever way the next tick happens to go.

  **Execution has the same idempotency guarantee as settlement**: the row is claimed with a conditional update *before* anything is placed, so two overlapping sweeps or a restart mid-flight can never open the same order twice — an integration test runs two sweeps concurrently and asserts exactly one position. A claimed order that then fails to open is recorded as `FAILED` with the reason and never retried: the trader asked for one position, not for repeated attempts at a price that has moved. Orders ride the settlement loop, reading prices from the feed, which knows nothing about orders — an order can no more move a price than a position can.

  **No money is held while an order waits**, because it is not a position yet. Whatever would refuse the trade at purchase — balance, a risk limit, a closed market — refuses it at triggering, and the reason is shown on the order. A market that has closed since the order was placed leaves it waiting rather than failing it. Orders carry a `goodUntil` (default 24h, configurable) so an unreached level is retired instead of waiting forever, and a per-trader cap on waiting orders.

  Both expiry modes work on an order: a duration is stored, and a clock boundary is re-validated when it fires because by then it may have passed. The terminal gains a Market/Pending switch with a price level seeded from the live tape (saying which way it will fire) or a start time, and the positions panel gains a proper `tablist` with a **Pending** tab that lists waiting orders apart from positions, each cancellable, with finished ones below showing why they failed. Every transition arrives over the socket, since an order changes state without the trader doing anything.

  **Found and fixed a real listing bug**: `listOrders` sorted by the status column, which is alphabetical — CANCELLED before PENDING — so a trader with a long history could have their live orders pushed past the limit entirely. Waiting orders now come first and whole, with a test that would catch it again.

  Verified against the running server: a price order placed just above the live BTC/USDT tape filled in about three seconds, and the position's entry price honoured the level. 15 unit tests for the trigger logic, 11 integration tests (including the concurrent-sweep guarantee, an IDOR check, the failure path and the cap), 2 e2e specs. 204 server + 24 web unit tests, 51 integration tests, lint, typecheck, format and build all clean.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 2 — **Expiry modes**, the first task of Phase 2. `engine/expiry.ts` is pure and resolves both modes, so the countdown a trader watches and the validation that accepts their trade come from the same arithmetic rather than two implementations that can drift. **Duration** is a fixed length from purchase, now configurable per market: `Asset.durations` narrows the platform list (and can only ever narrow it — a market cannot offer 7 seconds because the platform does not), falling back to the platform list when it is null or holds nothing usable. **Clock time** expires on the next boundary — 12:05, 12:10, 12:15 — from a configurable set of steps, with a purchase cut-off (default 30s) before each boundary, a horizon (default 4h) beyond which nothing is offered, and a live "time to buy" countdown per boundary. One instant that is a boundary of several steps collapses to a single slot keeping the coarsest step, because that is how a trader reads it ("the 13:00 expiry").

  The server validates both and trusts nothing from the client: a clock trade sends only the boundary, and its length is derived server-side from the instant the position actually opens. It refuses an instant that is not a boundary, one inside its cut-off, one already past, one beyond the horizon, one that is not a time at all, and a mode an operator has turned off. A test asserts the contract that matters — every slot the server *offers* is one it will *accept*.

  New in both modes: **a position may not outlive its market's session.** An expiry after the close has no tick to be priced from, so it is refused with a message pointing at the OTC twin. `Trade.expiryMode` records how a position was bought so history can show it.

  The ticket gains a mode switch (shown only when the platform offers both), the market's own duration list, and the clock boundaries with a per-slot countdown that turns red in the last ten seconds and drops the slot when it closes; it re-fetches from the server as the list ages rather than extrapolating in the browser.

  **Also fixed a real session bug found by the suite**: `web/src/lib/api.ts` cleared the tokens whenever a refresh came back not-ok, so a 429 from the rate limiter or a 502 from a restarting instance signed the user out for no reason. Only a 401 or 403 — a refusal of the token itself — ends a session now; two unit tests cover the rate-limited and restarting cases.

  20 unit tests for the expiry engine, 9 integration tests across both modes and the per-market list, 2 e2e specs (one buys a clock expiry and asserts the countdown actually counts down), plus five new runtime settings. 189 server + 24 web unit tests, 40 integration tests, lint, typecheck, format and build all clean.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 1 — **Risk limits**, which completes Phase 1. The house can now cap what it is on the hook for, the only honest way: **refuse a stake, never steer a quote.** `engine/risk.ts` is pure and, enforced structurally by a test, does not so much as mention a price, a payout, a tick, an outcome or settlement. Three limits per market — max stake per position (already there), max open stake one trader may hold on that market, and max open exposure the house will carry on one side — each with a platform-wide default in `risk.maxOpenStakePerUser` / `risk.maxExposurePerDirection` that a market's own figure overrides. A limit of 0 falls back to the default, and a default of 0 means no limit, so existing markets behave exactly as before until someone sets a number.

  The check runs *inside* the transaction that creates the position, so the aggregate it reads and the row it guards are one unit of work; two simultaneous trades can still overshoot a house limit by at most one stake, which is a better trade than serialising every trade on a whole market, and it is written down in the code. Rejections are specific and actionable rather than a flat refusal: "You can hold $150.00 open on this market. $50.00 left." and "This market has reached its limit on that side. Try the other direction or another market." The reason stays on the ticket until the trader changes the stake instead of disappearing with the toast.

  **Practice and tournament positions are never counted against the house** — that is not the house's money, and a practice trader must never be turned away because live traders filled a book. A trader's own per-market cap still applies within whichever account they are trading.

  A new admin **Risk** screen shows the live book, refreshing itself every five seconds: open stake per side per market with the room left on each, net direction, worst-case payout liability (the payout, not the stake — the stake is already the trader's), open positions and distinct traders, side utilisation, a banner naming every market at 80% of a side limit or more, and inline editing of all four limits with an audit row per change.

  15 unit tests for the limit arithmetic and the allowance calculation; 7 integration tests on the money path, including that a rejection leaves no position and moves no money, that a full UP book leaves DOWN untouched, that practice money stays out of the book, that the figures the back office shows match the trades that exist, and that a market's own limit wins over the platform default; 2 e2e specs driving an operator and a trader in separate contexts. 169 server + 22 web unit tests, 31 integration tests, lint, typecheck, format and build all clean.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 1 — **Payout engine**. A market's base payout can now be moved by configurable rules, and what they resolve to is locked into the trade row the moment it opens. `engine/payout.ts` is pure and, like the price engine, **cannot read a trader, a position or the house's exposure** — a payout belongs to the market and the moment, not to who is asking or what they hold. That is enforced the same two ways: a structural test asserting the module imports nothing and mentions none of `prisma`/`trade`/`position`/`exposure`/`userId`/`balance`, and a behavioural test that smuggles `openPositions`, `exposure`, `userId` and `winRate` into the input and proves the result is identical.

  Three rule kinds, each validated against its own zod shape: **time of day** (a daily UTC window, which may run past midnight — the Asian session is written 22:00→06:00, and a wrapping window belongs to the day it started on), **volatility** (fires when a market is moving more or less than a multiple of the volatility it is configured for, measured from the closed one-minute candles the feed already holds; a market with too little history to measure does not fire the rule rather than guessing), and **scheduled** (a dated window for a news release). A rule is scoped to one market, one asset class, or every market; rules apply in priority order and add up, and one marked exclusive stops the rest. The total is clamped by two new runtime settings, `trading.minPayoutPct` (20) and `trading.maxPayoutPct` (95). The Phase 4 status bonus is a parameter the resolver already takes and adds.

  Live everywhere it matters: the catalogue API returns the resolved payout with its base and the rules that moved it, a ws `payouts` message pushes the markets whose figure changed every ten seconds (with the reason, so the terminal can explain the number rather than just show it), and the ticket prints "▼ 87% base · Quiet Asian session" under the payout. A new admin **Payouts** screen creates, edits, enables and deletes rules, previews a rule against any market before it is saved, and lists every market currently paying something other than its base. Every mutation reloads the cache so a change is immediate, writes an `AuditLog` row, and the 60-second refresh exists only so a scheduled window can open on its own.

  25 unit tests for the engine and its config schemas; 4 integration tests on the money path (base locked when nothing fires, adjusted payout locked while positions already open keep theirs, settlement paying the locked figure after the rule changed, and the floor respected against an absurd −90 rule); 2 e2e specs, one driving an operator and a trader in separate browser contexts and checking the trader's ticket moves by exactly the rule's eleven points without a reload. Verified in a browser at 1440px and 390px with no console errors.

- **2026-09-17**: Phase 1 — **Candle storage**. Ticks now roll into durable candles at all 14 timeframes (5s → 1d) in a new `Candle` table keyed (symbol, timeframe, time), so a re-flushed bucket updates instead of duplicating. `engine/timeframes.ts` holds the timeframe table and its per-timeframe retention (6 hours for 5s, 10 years for 1d); `services/candles.ts` buffers closed candles and writes them as one multi-row upsert every 5s, prunes past retention every 10 minutes, and serves `history(symbol, timeframe, {before, limit})`. The history endpoint is paginated backwards — `{candles, nextBefore, price}` — and the chart pages as you scroll into the past (it pre-fetches when the view reaches within ten bars of the oldest one), with the quick timeframe row plus a menu covering all fourteen. The feed keeps only 60 candles per timeframe in memory now that the store owns history, and the ws snapshot reads from the store, so a restart draws real history instead of an empty chart.

  Generated history is its own problem and took the longest: a broker-priced market has no exchange archive, so `engine/history.ts` produces its past from the same engine that prices its present. Every page is walked with `elapsedMs === tickMs` over exactly one candle's worth of engine time (coarsened above 480 ticks, with the volatility scaled up to compensate) so a daily candle moves like a day; then it is pinned with a Brownian bridge to the price it joins — the newest page to the market's *live* price, each older page to the open of the oldest candle already stored — and its interior damped into a band that follows the market's own volatility and the span the page covers. Candle geometry is damped by the same factor rather than only the level, so a calmed page is a calmer walk and not a row of dojis with full-size wicks, and every open is stitched to the previous close, so a generated page has no gaps at all. Verified eight pages deep against the running server: AAPL 1m spans 2.4% over 40 hours and closes exactly on the live price, EUR/USD 0.7%, BTC/USDT 4.7%, gold 1d 25% over six years, 2,401 candles per market with no duplicates and the only discontinuity the 0.01–0.05% seam where generated history meets the live bucket. 18 unit tests for the generator, 5 for the timeframe table, 3 new feed tests and 11 integration tests across the store, deep paging and level stability, plus two e2e specs for scroll-back paging and the fourteen-timeframe menu.

  Retention bounds the table: fully populated — every market charted on every timeframe, a decade of daily candles — the 89 markets come to 6.1M rows, around 300MB, and pruning holds it there.

  Restart continuity needed two more pieces, both found by stopping a running server and comparing the tape: the store only ever heard about a candle when it *closed*, so the bucket open at shutdown was never written and the chart came back with a one-minute hole; and once written, the next process opened a fresh candle over the top of it, losing the bucket's original open and its high and low. The feed now exposes its open buckets, the store picks them up on every flush (so even a hard kill loses at most five seconds), and at boot the feed adopts whatever the last process was in the middle of printing. Verified across a real restart: the open bucket survived with its open preserved and its high and low intact, and the close simply moved on.

  Two real engine defects surfaced while measuring this, both now fixed with tests:

  - **The GARCH recursion was not normalised for elapsed time.** `lastShock` stored the printed return, but the variance it feeds is defined per `tickMs`; a tick covering more than one interval therefore fed back a proportionally larger shock, the recursion's effective coefficient passed 1, and the variance ran away. Realised volatility was 5.4× what the market's configuration claimed. `lastShock` is now normalised to one interval, and a test asserts realised volatility tracks the square-root law at 1, 1.5 and 3 intervals per tick.
  - **Drift and mean reversion were expressed per tick, so the engine's behaviour depended on its own tick size.** A trend accumulated linearly in tick count while volatility grows with its root, so the same configuration trended forty times as far at a quarter-second tick as at a ten-second one: AAPL swung 10% in an hour on a market whose volatility says 0.7%, then sat flat over days. `trendStrength` is now a multiple of the move a regime's own duration implies (divided by the root of its length, which needed the regime's full length in the persisted state — new column, migrated), and `meanReversion` is the share of the gap to the anchor closed per minute. The same defaults now produce 0.6% an hour and 1.7% a day for AAPL against a 0.7%/3.4% square-root reference, and a test asserts the same configuration produces the same market at 250ms, 1s and 10s ticks. Spot-following OTC twins keep up with their exchange market on a time basis too (4× per minute anchor tracking, with a reversion floor), instead of a rate that only made sense at one tick size. Admin help text for both parameters updated to their new meaning.

## Decisions

_Record product choices made without the owner here: what, why, where it's configurable._

- **Hotkey preferences live in the browser, not the account.** They are a per-device convenience — a trader on a laptop and a phone wants different things, and a key map is not worth a round trip or a table. The platform switch stays server-side in settings.
- **Repeating a position resolves its expiry server-side.** A client cannot send the original's expiry, because a clock boundary has passed by the time the button is clicked and a duration may have been withdrawn from the market. A duration repeat keeps its length or is refused; a clock repeat takes the soonest boundary still open.
- **A market's stake range wins over the platform's presets.** Presets and balance shares outside a market's min/max are hidden or disabled rather than shown and then refused by the server.
- **A pending order holds no money while it waits.** It is not a position, so it is funded and risk-checked at the moment it fires; a refusal then is recorded on the order with its reason rather than reserving a balance for hours. This matches how Quotex treats them and keeps the ledger free of holds that are not positions.
- **A price order's side is fixed when it is placed**, from the price at that instant, and stored. An order cannot be re-interpreted later, which is what stops it firing the wrong way after the price has crossed and returned.
- **A failed trigger is final, not retried.** The trader asked for one position at a level; retrying at a price that has since moved would be a different trade than the one they asked for.
- **A market may narrow the platform's expiry list, never widen it.** `Asset.durations` is filtered against the platform list, so an operator cannot create a per-market expiry the settings do not offer, and a list with nothing usable falls back rather than leaving a market untradeable.
- **A position may not expire after its market closes**, in either mode. There would be no tick to price it from; the refusal points at the OTC twin, which never closes.
- **A clock trade sends only the boundary.** The position's length is derived server-side from the instant it opens, so a client cannot claim a duration that does not match its expiry.
- **Risk limits count live money only.** Practice and tournament stakes are not the house's exposure, so they never enter the book and never block a practice trader. A trader's own per-market cap still applies inside whichever account they trade.
- **Risk is checked inside the position's transaction**, not with a market-wide lock. Two simultaneous trades can overshoot a house limit by at most one stake; serialising every trade on a market to close that window would cost far more than it saves.
- **No payout rules are seeded.** A fresh install pays every market its base payout, which is the neutral, predictable default; the admin Payouts screen explains itself with an empty state and a preview, so an operator can build the first rule and see its effect before saving it.
- **Payout rules are blind to traders by design.** They read the clock, the calendar and how much a market is moving — never a position, an exposure or a user's history. Same hard rule as the price engine, enforced by the same structural and behavioural tests. Risk controls (next task) limit *new stakes*; nothing steers a quote or a payout toward an individual.
- **Volatility thresholds are ratios, not absolute moves.** "Moving more than 1.5× its normal" works for a rule scoped to every market, where an absolute per-minute figure would only suit one. A market without enough history to measure does not fire the rule.
- **E2E browser binary**: `playwright.config.ts` honours `E2E_CHROMIUM_PATH` so sandboxes with a pre-installed Chromium don't download another. CI installs the pinned browser and ignores the variable.
- **Prettier scope**: code only. `*.md` is ignored so the owner's docs keep their own formatting.
- **Settings vs env**: env stays the bootstrap (DSN, secrets, ports, feed provider) and seeds the *defaults* of runtime keys; anything an operator would change while running lives in the settings registry. Existing deployments keep their current behaviour until someone edits a value in admin.
- **Live data is opt-in**: `FEED_PROVIDER=simulated` (the default) keeps every market broker-priced. The gate lives in the feed, not the registry, so the registry stays pure provider plumbing and is testable without env stubbing.
- **Forex/stock quotes**: implemented against a Twelve Data-shaped `/price` endpoint because it has a usable free tier, but `MARKET_DATA_URL` accepts any service with the same response shape. Real credentials are the owner's to supply.
- **One engine for every simulated market**: rather than keep the old random walk for exchange-traded markets and add a second engine for OTC, all simulated pricing runs through the OTC engine. Non-OTC markets keep their session gating and will be driven by a real provider in the "Real feeds" task; OTC markets are broker-priced by definition.
- **RNG state column**: mulberry32's state is a uint32, which does not fit MySQL's signed `INT`. The column is `INT UNSIGNED` (found by running it — the first flush failed with "out of range").
- **Session ownership**: calendars are shared records (forex, metals/energy, US equities, US/European index CFDs) rather than per-asset hours, so one edit moves every market on that venue. Crypto and OTC deliberately carry no calendar.
- **Closed markets freeze**: rather than keep generating candles nobody can trade, the feed skips markets outside their session. The last tick stands until the market reopens.
- **Crypto quote currency**: the original markets were BTC/USD; the catalogue quotes crypto in USDT because that is what the exchange feed prices. A data migration renamed the assets and their denormalised trade symbols rather than leaving two conventions in the table.
- **OTC pricing premium**: OTC twins get +3% payout (capped at 95%) and 1.15× volatility, mirroring how brokers price their own books. Both numbers live in `data/markets.ts` and become per-asset admin settings in the OTC engine task.
- **Practice starting balance** moved to `trading.practiceStartBalance` (default $10,000) and the refill threshold to `trading.practiceRefillBelow` (0 = always allowed), matching Quotex's refillable demo account.
